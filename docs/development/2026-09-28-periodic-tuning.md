# Periodic velocity-loop identification

The initial implementation and first run below use protocol version 1. The
current version 2 changes and diagnostic are described in the final section.

The J1 PRBS experiment on September 27 rejected three attempts without tuning:
the first reversed the slide, and the lower-amplitude pair did not cover the
crossover. This implementation changes the experiment and estimator while
retaining the six-attempt maximum per joint and the existing gain validation.

## Experiment

Each pose has two multisine recordings. Frequencies are integer multiples of
the period frequency. Schroeder phases reduce the waveform peak; fixed Q15
tables are generated by `scripts/periodic_waveforms.py` and interpolated using
integer arithmetic in the 6250 Hz firmware loop. The command specifies a peak
bound in mA, not RMS. No trigonometry or allocation runs in the firmware ISR.

| Profile | Frequencies | Sample divisor | Period | Settling + recording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 3.05–61.04 Hz | 16 | 0.65536 s | 1.31072 + 2.62144 s |
| High | 36.62–701.90 Hz | 4 | 0.16384 s | 0.32768 + 0.65536 s |

Each recording contains four complete periods of 256 samples, following two
unrecorded settling periods. The buffers remain 1024 samples per channel.
The host merges the responses at 48 Hz. These frequencies, settling periods,
amplitude and sample counts are engineering choices requiring hardware
validation, not quantities guaranteed by the cited papers.

Both profiles use the same slide direction and speed at a pose. The host
chooses up to 7500 motor ticks/s with the longest experiment and both septic
speed ramps inside 60% of the available travel. It refuses a slide below 1250
ticks/s rather than continuing near friction reversal. The starting peak is
5% of the joint current limit, scaled down with available sliding speed and
capped at 1000 mA; if this leaves less than 20 mA, the pose is refused. A reversal consumes one of two shared retries and halves
that peak for subsequent attempts. Actual travel beyond 80% of the available
arc or the existing runaway-speed threshold cancels the experiment.

## Estimator and acceptance

The synchronous DFT uses complete periods without Hann windowing. A linear background is fitted from whole-period means
to remove gradual current/speed bias without using the excited harmonics to
estimate that slope. This assumes the background is locally linear.
Cross spectra against the known excitation are averaged at each individual
frequency across the four periods. Neighboring frequencies are never pooled.
Period scatter estimates uncertainty in the plant ratio, including covariance
between current and velocity through the residual `W - P U`. A uniform
quantization floor covers the recorded channel units; that floor is not
divided by the number of repeated periods. It also prevents a repeatable
quantized zero from reporting high coherence.

Gain design uses a plant uncertainty radius of two standard errors. It reduces
the Nyquist distance by `|C| radius` when evaluating sensitivity and widens the
crossover coverage checks. This is a conservative numerical guard under the
noise assumptions, not a statistical stability certificate. Four periods cannot
identify every systematic distortion, phase-locked disturbance, or changing
mechanical configuration. The configured PI consistency check and the existing
coherence, sensitivity, hold and directional tracking requirements remain.
Only one candidate is physically compared to baseline, with two directions
for each: at most four validation steps and two holds.

References:

- [Schoukens, Godfrey and Schoukens, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCS.2018.2830080):
  periodic excitation, reference channels in closed-loop identification, and
  measurement uncertainty.
- [Schroeder, 1970](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054411): low-peak-factor
  multisine phase selection.
- [Åström, Panagopoulos and Hägglund, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0005-1098(98)00011-9):
  PI integral-action optimization subject to sensitivity constraints.

## Firmware contract

STEPFOC command 43, protocol version 1, uses an 8-byte data frame:
`version, profile, divisor, zero, token_hi, token_lo, peak_hi, peak_lo`.
The token must be nonzero. Profiles 0/1 require divisors 16/4 respectively.
Peak zero cancels. An RTR replies with the same layout, replacing byte 3 with
state flags: armed=1, running=2, complete=4, aborted=8, clipped=16. This query
never enables injection. The host requires support before startup current or
homing, requires an armed acknowledgement before sliding, and verifies the
complete experiment identity before accepting its samples. Older firmware
cannot silently fall back to PRBS or electrical-phase capture.

Command 38 starts the armed experiment and must request exactly 1024 samples
with the matching divisor. The firmware discards the first two periods and
stops excitation after the following four. A fault, departure from velocity
mode, replacement capture, or explicit cancellation stops excitation. Current
within 5% of the limit latches the clipped flag, including during settling.
Command 39 still downloads the usual channels: 0 velocity, 2 current setpoint.
Version the protocol again when changing tables or sampling conventions.

Firmware source is preserved in `/home/rbt/stepfoc-firmware/source`, based on
upstream commit `46b4911dd8f7e082a4ef39f13f736784647156ba` plus the earlier PAR6
firmware changes. No electrical calibration or EEPROM layout is changed.

## Focused diagnostic (protocol version 2)

The first recording gave approximately 2.2 mA RMS per low-band frequency,
while a nonstationary speed component near 19 Hz dominated the response. Its
cause is unresolved; those recordings cannot distinguish a fixed resonance
from speed-dependent disturbances. No conclusion about optimal gains follows.

Version 2 retains the same periods, settling, samples, divisors and frequency
grid. Low-profile line weights are 1 in 10–40 Hz, 0.5 below it and 0.25 above
it. Phase increments follow cumulative line power (equal weights recover the
original Schroeder phases). This places about 90% of line power in 10–40 Hz
while retaining all anchor frequencies and the 1.53 Hz spacing through the
19 Hz component. The high profile retains equal weights. Both consumers and
the firmware protocol version change together; a v1 status is rejected.

The host first moves to 10% inside the nearer end of the already checked
interval. The longer available arc allows a faster slide under the same
60% planned-travel and 80% measured-travel bounds. Every successful or
measurement-rejected acquisition returns to the original planned pose before
following its collision-checked route back to ready. Faults use the existing
shutdown path. This remains a local-response experiment, not a certificate
that inertia is constant throughout the slide.

Normal tuning records the low band first. Before spending the high-band
record it requires every 10–40 Hz line to have coherence >= 0.6 and a plant
uncertainty radius <= 35% of its magnitude, plus the existing PI consistency
check. The 35% threshold is an engineering measurement gate. Full crossover
coverage and the existing gain-design/validation requirements still apply.
The normal maximum remains six identification attempts per joint.

`--only gains --joint 1 --gains-diagnostic` selects the first planned pose and
at most two low-band records. It has no retries, high-band captures, gain
changes or validation steps, and rejects `--apply`, `--limits`, additional
stages and multiple joints. The first amplitude uses the existing 5%-of-limit
ceiling scaled with slide speed. The second is 1.5 times the first, capped at
5% of the current limit. It is attempted only if scaling all observed speed
deviation by 1.5 still leaves one filtered-speed quantum above reversal and
scaling peak current by 1.5 remains below 95% of the current limit. This is a
conservative headroom check, not a prediction of the nonlinear response.

The diagnostic reports usable line counts and controller errors. It compares
the two responses only if both meet the quality gate; agreement means their
complex plant uncertainty disks overlap at every 10–40 Hz line. A weak estimate
cannot qualify merely because its uncertainty is large. Agreement still does
not establish full tuning coverage or optimal gains.

Verification: six targeted Rust tests covering actual hardware replay, Fourier
algebra, amplitude agreement/disagreement, invalid intervals, wire identity and
host/firmware samples; the native firmware lifecycle test; Clippy with warnings
denied; Rust/Python formatting and Python lint; firmware and host release builds.
No simulator was used. Artifacts are `stepfoc-V108-par6-v8-focused.bin` and its
JSON manifest under `/home/rbt/stepfoc-firmware/images`, with the complete
`stepfoc-par6-v8-focused.patch` checked against the upstream revision.

### What the partial measurement says about the current gains

The overall rejection does not mean the captures contain no evidence about
the existing tuning. At 18.31 and 19.84 Hz, the two captures have usable plant
estimates that agree across amplitudes. Their pointwise PI discrepancies are
only 0.5–2.7%, although the broad-band median printed by the diagnostic is
27.6–44.7%. That median also includes coherent frequencies with large plant
uncertainty; it must not be described as a controller mismatch at the reliable
18–20 Hz lines.

An offline check directly estimates the current-injection sensitivity as the
mean current-setpoint Fourier coefficient divided by the known injection
coefficient, `U/D`. The same whole-period background removal is used. With a
constant speed reference and feedforward, linear closed-loop theory gives
`U/D = 1/(1+PC)`; see the
[Lund control exercises, sensitivity function](https://control.lth.se/fileadmin/control/Education/EngineeringProgram/FRTF05/exercises_print.pdf).
This is an inference about the physical loop under the local linear and noise
assumptions, not a global stability certificate.

| Frequency | 47 mA: magnitude ± two-SE radius | 71 mA: magnitude ± two-SE radius |
|---|---:|---:|
| 18.31 Hz | 6.79 ± 2.11 | 6.16 ± 1.93 |
| 19.84 Hz | 9.60 ± 4.98 | 8.87 ± 3.44 |

The radius is twice the standard error of the complex period responses, with
the current quantization floor retained. It is the same engineering uncertainty
convention as the estimator, not a guaranteed confidence interval. Both
amplitudes provide evidence of a large resonant peak at the current gains,
well beyond the configured sensitivity target of 1.5. That identifies a
specific weakness in the current tuning at this operating point. It does not
establish that another PI pair meets all requirements, nor select replacement
gains without the missing frequency coverage and physical comparison.
